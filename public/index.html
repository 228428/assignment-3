Who Are the Brokpas?

The Brokpas are Bhutan’s semi-nomadic highland people who inhabit the remote valleys of Merak and Sakteng in Trashigang District. Living between 3,000 and 4,000 meters above sea level, they are known for their yak herding traditions and unique language, Brokkat, which is spoken only within their community. Their livelihood depends on yaks for food, clothing, and trade. The Brokpas’ woven wool garments, yak-hair hats, and seasonal movements reflect a deep connection to nature and to Bhutan’s ancestral roots. Their culture symbolizes Bhutan’s resilience — balancing modern progress while protecting mountain traditions.

Threads of Identity

The Brokpa weaving tradition is a living archive of heritage. Each pattern carries meaning — the diamond motifs represent protection, the zigzags mirror mountain ridges, and the bold stripes signal lineage. Yak wool is hand-spun into thread, dyed using natural pigments from roots and minerals, and woven on backstrap looms. The process is communal — women gather to weave and share stories passed through generations. The weaves express identity and emotion: strength in red, purity in white, endurance in black. Every cloth is both practical and spiritual, protecting against the highland chill while symbolizing a bond between people, animals, and land.

Early Footsteps

Herding begins in childhood. Brokpa children learn to read the weather, follow yak bells, and understand the rhythm of the land. The first lesson is patience — moving at the pace of wind and herd.

Learning the Craft

Young Brokpas learn to spin yak wool, weave fabric, and make butter and cheese. These tasks sustain families through harsh winters. Skills are shared across generations through observation, rhythm, and repetition.

Seasons and Routes

The Brokpas migrate with their yaks according to the seasons. In summer, herds graze in the high pastures. In winter, they descend to lower valleys. These migrations keep the ecosystem balanced and ensure sustainability for both people and animals. Yaks provide milk, transport, wool, and are essential to Brokpa survival.

Festivals and Belonging

During festivals, families reunite to dance, sing, and share food made from yak butter and cheese. The celebrations reinforce belonging, marking the cycles of time and gratitude for the mountains. The Brokpas celebrate their identity not in words, but in rhythm and color.

New Paths

As education reaches the highlands, younger generations pursue studies in the cities. They seek stability and modern livelihoods, yet carry their homeland in stories and dress. The challenge is to sustain traditions in a rapidly changing world.

Hands at the Loom

Every Brokpa cloth is a conversation between generations. The loom hums with memory — a form of storytelling beyond words. These weaves preserve not only warmth, but identity.

Memory Maps

Hand-drawn routes show how families once moved with their herds. They serve as memory maps, marking sacred mountains, river crossings, and pasture zones. These maps are now being digitized to protect intangible heritage.

Seasons of Change

Snow patterns shift and grass grows differently than before. Climate change challenges the herding lifestyle. The Brokpas adapt with resilience, finding new balance between tradition and modernity.
